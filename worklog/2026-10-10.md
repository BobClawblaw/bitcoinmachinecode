## The delete path timed in parts: a transaction's outputs in neighbouring slots

Run 46's `put.del` was ~480 ns per spent input over 575k-875k, about half of
`put`. A 60 s perf of the applier (pdiag, main's code, ~740k) left most of it
unattributed -- the asm has no unwind info and `memcmp_asm`/`utxo_hash` are
shared with the lookups -- so a diagnostic branch (40cbd3c1, not merged)
timed `utxo_lsm_del` in parts with `rdtsc` and counted the probes, on a fresh
sync (pdiag2). Over 575k-793k, 1.2 billion deletes, 79% of them memtable hits:

| part | ns per delete |
|------|---------------|
| WAL append | 16 |
| memtable delete | 190 |
| tombstone step | 124 |

330 of the bench line's 478 ns; the rest is the call and the timer brackets.
A hit's backward shift scanned 10.8 slots, re-hashing each entry (an 8-step
FNV byte loop and a `div`) and moving 2.9 of them with a per-byte copy; the
tombstone probe took 1-6 steps a call, each a random read of `tomb_buf`.

Cause: the memtable, the tombstone set and the in-block index all placed a
key at FNV(txid[0..8]) XOR index under the mask. The index flips only low
bits, so a transaction's outputs sat side by side and every multi-output
transaction made a run of full slots, which linear probing and the backward
shift walk. The FNV part alone spreads evenly (2^23 random txids: 6,600,357
distinct home slots, uniform expectation 6,601,320).

The fix (branch perf/2026-10-10-memtable-hash):
- `utxo_hash`, the tombstone probe and `outpoint_hash` (the in-block index):
  (txid bytes 0..7 XOR index) times 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15, bits 32.. under the
  mask. The txid needs no hashing of its own; the multiply carries the index
  into every bit above it. Nothing persists a slot position: the checkpoint and
  the WAL hold records, reload re-puts them, the tombstone set is rebuilt from
  `tomb_buf`, the frozen copy is probed by the same functions. The Bloom hash
  (`mac_bloom_h`) is in the run files and is unchanged.
- The backward shift computes each entry's home slot inline (no call, no
  `*48`, no `div`).
- `memcpy_asm` and `mac_memcpy` are `rep movsb` (FSRM), `memcmp_asm` compares
  8 bytes at a time; all three were per-byte loops (~6% of the applier's CPU).
