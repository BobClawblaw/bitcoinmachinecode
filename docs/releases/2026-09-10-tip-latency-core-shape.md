# 2026-09-10 — At the tip, Core's shape: announcements drive the pass, high-bandwidth compact blocks, the apply follows the store

"Resolve those identified items to be in line with core behavior." Inventory rows 1 and 2 (header announcements, high-bandwidth compact blocks) plus the apply-after-store piece of row 3.

**Measured first**, production on snapshot p against the Core oracle on the same box, its UpdateTip line against our "new block" line: 5 to 20 s behind on every block of the evening. A passive capture on one leg (a Hetzner peer with a 0.8 s round trip) split one block up: getheaders 0.8 s, getdata to cmpctblock 0.9 s, then 1.4 s until our getblocktxn, 0.9 s to the blocktxn's first byte, 4.6 s until the pass's next getheaders. The two long gaps are not our CPU: they are TCP slow start after idle on a long-RTT peer, a 40 KB compact block in three round trips and an 800 KB blocktxn in seven. Half the block's transactions were missing from our mempool, which is why the blocktxn was 800 KB of a 1.6 MB block. Then the stored block waited up to 9 s for the rest of the rotation before the apply ran.

**What Core does:** sends sendheaders after verack and is told of new blocks by pushed headers; sends getheaders only with cause; makes the three peers that most recently delivered a block high-bandwidth compact-block sources, which push the block with no announcement and no round trip; connects a block the moment it has it.

- **The sweep recognises block traffic** (`tx_relay.c`): an inv naming a block, a pushed `headers`, and a `cmpctblock`, `blocktxn` or `block` reach the daemon's hooks instead of the discard. Block-relay-only legs get a blocks-only sweep of their own (they used to be swept for nothing, so their announcements were lost). The payload buffer grew to hold a pushed blocktxn or block (4 MB).
- **sendheaders after the handshake**, gated like sendcmpct on the receive side being installed, so the sync harnesses' fake peers see the bare stream.
- **A leg with an announced block we do not have goes next in the rotation** (`leg_announced_pick`), and **a leg is not polled for headers otherwise**: a pass runs on an announcement, or after 30 s as the safety net. Production sent one getheaders per leg per rotation, a few a second across the pool.
- **The three most recent block sources are high-bandwidth** (`leg_hb_note_block`): sendcmpct with the flag set to the leg that just delivered a block, the oldest of four back to low bandwidth. Their pushed cmpctblock is reconstructed from the sweep against the shared mempool and, on our tip, stored through the evaluator submitblock uses (PoW, cons_verify) and the locked append; a getblocktxn round trip and a full-block fallback come back through the same sweep. One in-flight claim per block keeps a push and a sibling's pass from fetching it twice; a push that is not on our tip marks the leg announced and the pass sorts it out.
- **A store ends the rotation**: the apply, the header mirror and the new-block choke run at once; the rotation resumes after that leg.

**Verified:** `test_tx_relay` scenario 9 drives every hook over a socketpair (a block inv among tx entries, a pushed header with its bytes intact, cmpctblock, blocktxn not swallowed as a block, a pushed block; the blocks-only sweep answers a ping and ignores a tx inv). `validation/cmpct_regtest_e2e.sh` with Core's net logging: Core received our high-bandwidth sendcmpct after the first block it delivered and pushed a cmpctblock for each of the six that followed; every one was stored as "pushed compact block + blocktxn" with no pass, and bmc followed Core to 116 of 116.

**Still open, found by the capture:** mempool overlap. A blocktxn half the size of the block means our mempool holds far fewer of the transactions Core's peers relay than Core's does (4,472 entries on production against a network mempool of tens of thousands). That is the next divergence row; the operator may also consider `net.ipv4.tcp_slow_start_after_idle=0` on the host, which is what makes the second and third round trips of a push expensive, and is Core's environment as much as ours.

**To measure on production:** the UpdateTip-vs-new-block gap against the oracle over an evening (expected under 2 s on a low-RTT source), the `[cmpct]` high-bandwidth lines, "pushed compact block" stores, and the getheaders count per hour on a leg's capture.
