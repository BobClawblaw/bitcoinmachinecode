# 2026-09-10 — The parallel download takes Core's shape

Row 1 of `docs/CORE_DIVERGENCES.md` (download parallelism). "Implement Core's shape." Core's block download during initial sync: every usable peer downloads, up to 16 blocks in flight each; blocks are requested only inside a 1,024-block window above the connected tip; assignment follows completion, with no rate estimate and no ranking; the one judge of slowness is the window's tail, where the peer holding the oldest missing block while the window is full has 2 s (doubling to 64 s when evictions come fast, easing back as blocks land) before it is disconnected and its block re-requested.

Ours, on run 19: 16 chunk workers of a fixed count against 124 confirmed-live peers, 11.3 MB/s on a 2.5 Gbit link (the sum of 16 peers at a 366 KB/s median), a 4,096-block window above the download's own frontier while the apply trailed it by up to 969 blocks, an EMA ranking to pick replacements, a rate floor relative to the median, and an idle worker fetching the blocking chunk itself as a duplicate.

- **Every live peer downloads**, up to `bmc.catchupworkers`, whose meaning is now "at most this many peers at once" and whose default is 64 (the arrays' size; `dlc_workers_for`). The probe targets two live peers per slot instead of six. Each worker keeps a 40-block chunk in flight, more than Core's 16, so the in-flight budget per peer is not the lever here.
- **The window scales and anchors to the connected tip.** `dlc_window_blocks`: six times what is in flight (Core's ratio, 1,024 over about 160), never under 4,096; 64 workers give 15,360. `dlc_window_anchor`: the connected tip plus one when the UTXO engine is in the process, the archive's first hole otherwise. The download can no longer run further ahead of the apply than the window, which is what makes the apply the pacing element, as in Core.
- **The window's tail is the judge** (`dlc_stall_tick`, every 200 ms and once per connect pass): the committer's cursor chunk is the tail; while the window is full, the chunk is not staged, and a live worker holds it, that worker has the stall timeout before it is dropped with the labelled reason "stalling the window". The chunk goes to the retry ring, which the workers idle at the window are already polling. The timeout doubles on an eviction (cap 64 s) and eases 15% when the tail moves (floor 2 s), Core's adaptation. The worker-side help, a duplicate fetch after 2 s at the window, is gone; the committer's cursor help stays as the safety net for a chunk nobody holds.
- **Replacement only when a replacement exists.** Boundary rotation (under half the median) and the rate floor act only while an unclaimed usable peer is free; with every live peer employed, dropping a slow but delivering peer loses its contribution, and Core never does it. The tick line prints the free-peer count, the stall evictions and the current timeout.

Memory: each worker touches up to 32 MB of static buffers, so 64 of them can reach about 2 GB. The boot line prints the shape: "N of M live peer(s) download at once (cap C), window W blocks above the connected tip, stall timeout T s".

Tests: `test_dlc_rules` pins the pure rules (worker count, window size and anchor, the adaptive timeout, the stall predicate, the replacement gate). `test_dialhelper` drives `dlc_stall_tick` with a forked holder and a clock it controls: no eviction while the window has room, none while the tail chunk is staged, none at 1.9 s, the eviction at 2 s with the labelled reason and the doubled timeout, the easing when the tail moves, nothing when nobody holds the tail. Existing scenarios updated for the window's third parameter.

**To measure on the next fresh run:** aggregate receive rate against run 19's 11.3 MB/s, the 634,561 and 800,000 marks (run 19: 6h52m; Core 6h55m and 12h06m), the apply lag on the progress lines (the window should hold it under 15,360 and the apply should be the bound), stall evictions per hour, and the peer table's free-peer count. If the aggregate does not rise with the peer count, the pool's tail is the bound and the next lever is Core's per-peer in-flight budget through more peers, not deeper chunks.
