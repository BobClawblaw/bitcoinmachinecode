# 2026-09-09 — Chain selection like Core's: a handoff for long replacements, the header mirror as the best chain, a fork tree for every other branch

The bench finished a headers-first sync on a real 8-block stale branch at 961,632 (the oracle's `getchaintips` holds it as a headers-only fork; a peer stuck there served the header chain) and sat for four hours. Three defects, one incident: the reorg probe staged its replacement from the one peer that announced it, under the leg's 60 s budget, and the replacement was 4,500 blocks ("did not deliver replacement block 19 -- aborting BEFORE any change", every 30 s); the header sync read every fork-point reply as "already current"; and once a handoff rewound the node, a keep-up leg stored the same eight blocks again from the same peer thirty seconds later, because nothing remembered the branch had just lost on work. The operator's rule: do what Core does, retain both branches' headers.

- **Handoff.** Above `REORG_STAGE_MAX` (32, about what a leg delivers in 60 s) the probe rewinds to the fork point on the strength of the candidate's headers -- PoW-checked, heavier than ours, above -minimumchainwork, the evidence Core switches its header chain on -- through `reorg_disconnect_to`, the path invalidateblock already uses; appends the winner's headers to the header mirror; returns 3. The worker re-anchors its legs and runs the parallel downloader at once.
- **The header mirror is the best header chain.** headers.dat carries the heaviest known chain, ahead of the archive when a handoff or a fetch has proven more.
- **The fork tree** (`daemon/hdr_tree.c`, `headers_forks.dat`) retains every branch off the best chain with its cumulative work, as Core's block index keeps every valid header: the branch a handoff leaves, a probe's lighter candidate, a header page refused for forking below the tip. Bounded at 8,192 entries, the lightest tip evicted first, pruned below tip minus 20,000 at the heartbeat.
- **The leg gate.** After a keep-up leg stores blocks, any at a height the mirror knows under a different hash are off the best chain: retained in the tree, rewound, the leg re-anchored, the downloader told to run.

`test_hdr_tree` (new). `test_reorg`: a 10-block replacement above a staging limit of 8 hands off (**watched to fail**, 11 assertions, the old full reorg); the branch left is in the tree and the winner's headers in the mirror; a leg re-storing the stale block is gated back to the fork; a lighter competing chain is retained with no action. Gate `make -j8 test`: MAKE_EXIT 0, 0 failures (the expected test_rpc_signer segfault); audits exit 0.

**Live on the bench:** on the handoff-only build the probe rewound the eight blocks two minutes after boot, and a leg re-adopted them thirty seconds later -- the finding behind the fork tree and the gate. On the full build the handoff fired, the downloader fetched three thousand mainnet blocks from the fork point, and the engine then rejected mainnet's 963,311 for spending a coin the bench's set lacked -- a UTXO set left inconsistent by the earlier cycles on three binaries (the coin was created at 962,871, a block the bench applied on this run; the count equalled the oracle's). That inconsistency is the next investigation; the run itself is over, its logs kept under `bench-logs/run18-20260909`.
